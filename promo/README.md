## Words for the launch

**One line**

Jharokha: a display typeface with Latin letters built like Mughal architecture.

**Short post**

Meet Jharokha, a new display typeface. Every letter is a small building:
n, m and h stand on onion-arch doorways, round letters have bud-shaped
windows, and tall stems end in minaret domes. 210 glyphs, including the
rupee sign and the accents to write Sanskrit in Latin letters. Free and open
source under the SIL Open Font License.

**Longer**

A jharokha is the overhanging stone balcony of a Rajasthani or Mughal
palace. This typeface borrows its parts. The lowercase n is a block with a
pointed doorway cut into it. The o is a block with a window shaped like a
lotus bud. The i wears an onion dome, and so does every full stop. Capital
A, H and M have scalloped arches, and T, L and F rest on small brackets.

Jharokha is one heavy weight, made for headlines, posters, packaging and
signs. It covers A to Z, figures, punctuation, the rupee sign, Western
European accents, and the dotted and barred letters used to write Sanskrit
and other Indian languages in Latin script.

Jharokha is free to use, including commercially, under the SIL Open Font
License 1.1.
